sim-v6: choosing how to finish the study

A decision run. Three options, one recommendation, and the RQ2 power estimate that v5 could not provide.

1. Why this run exists

Collection paused at roughly 80 retained subjects, 39 and 39 across the two SHINE cohorts, on a three-way choice about how to spend the remaining budget. All three keep the random allocation arm, which is what is deployed and is not being revisited.

Everything decided so far rests on the v5 sweep, and v5 has four problems for this particular purpose. It never simulated these three cells directly. Its participants were calibrated on 41 pilot subjects running task versions 1.0–3.06, whose fitted noise scale is substantially too high for the deployed cohort. Its ground truth exceeds its own data's reliability ceiling, so part of its fine structure is fitted noise. And it has no RQ2 power estimate at all — the numbers produced earlier were an analytic approximation resting on a variance-scaling exponent that could not be justified.

This run is deliberately small: roughly 3,600 MDS fits against v5's 17,280. It is not a survey and should not be read as one.

2. What differs from v5

  1. Participants are recalibrated on production — all 84 candidates who sat the deployed task, not the 41 pilot subjects. The fitted noise scale falls from 0.30 to 0.22 and the population's spread from σ = 0.35 to 0.25: the deployed cohort is materially cleaner than the pilot was.
  2. The ground truth is rebuilt from the pilot plus the eight pre-SHINE production subjects the analysis discards anyway. Not circular: every one of them is excluded by a rule fixed before this analysis, so no subject who appears in the study's results contributes to the ground truth its design was chosen against.
  3. Retained cohorts exclude false alarms, matching the analysis that would actually be run rather than the task's own one-shot gate.
  4. RQ2 power is measured rather than approximated, by simulating cohorts on deliberately perturbed ground truths.
  5. Within-session drift is swept, not assumed. See section 4.

Everything else is v5 and is cited rather than repeated.

3. Does the model match what we can already see?

The recalibrated model has to reproduce the quantities production already shows before its predictions about the cells we cannot measure are worth anything. Six checks, all measured on the same 84 candidates, all passed.

Two of the six deserve a caveat rather than credit. The pass rates are the quantities the calibration was fitted against, so they are close to circular; the informative rows are retained test-retest, the false-positive rate and between-subject agreement, none of which the fit targeted directly.

4. On within-session drift

Production shows something v5's model cannot produce: every move-ratio failure in the live data falls in the experimental block and none in the screening block. Participants get worse after the gate has read them, and the deployed design is structurally blind to it. A single scalar multiplying a subject's noise in the second block is the smallest thing that can carry that.

The gate passes at both drift = 1.0 (none) and drift = 1.1 (the value fitted to production's 14.1% false-alarm rate), so the data does not settle the question. It is swept rather than chosen because the choice is not neutral for the decision: drift lowers retained reliability and raises the false-alarm rate, making every recovery metric worse and every retained subject more expensive, which pushes the answer toward the options that cost more. Picking one value by hand would tilt a spending decision on an assumption the data cannot support.

6. RQ2 power

RQ2a(ii) asks whether ρ(pre, post) sits below the within-condition ceiling. The null is free: two cohorts drawn from the same ground truth is exactly what the main sweep already produces. The alternative needs cohorts on a ground truth a known distance away, which is what the perturbation arm supplies. Power is then the share of alternative draws below the null's 5th percentile — no normal approximation, no attenuation formula.

8. What this run still cannot settle

  • The rebuilt ground truth is still mostly the pilot — 49 subjects rather than 41, and the eight added are the weakest available. Coverage and replication improve; whether the geometry is more trustworthy is checked by a one-sided gate, not guaranteed.
  • The RQ2 effect is isotropic by construction. If SHINE acts selectively on sensory dimensions, as RQ3 supposes, this estimate shifts depending on how much distance variance those dimensions carry. A sensory-weighted variant is the natural follow-up.
  • Drift is a description, not a mechanism. Fatigue, disengagement and strategy change are indistinguishable here, and the scalar claims only a direction and a rough size.
  • The false-alarm rate under drift = 1.1 is a fitted target, not a prediction. It must not be read as corroboration of the model under that setting.
